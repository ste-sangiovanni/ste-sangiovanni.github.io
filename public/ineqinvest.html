Three research trajectories

Citizens do not confront long-term policy choices from the same socioeconomic position.

Shared future goals conceal conflict over means, representation, and delivery.

1   ACTOR COMPETITION

When does future rhetoric
become policy commitment?

When is future-oriented language coupled with substantive policy instruments or tradeoffs, and when does it function mainly as a valence-based claim?

  • Future rhetoric versus concrete commitments
  • Policy-based valence and positional conflict
  • Who commits, when, and under which political conditions?
2   UNEQUAL REPRESENTATION

Whose future is
represented?

Which citizens’ long-term priorities are closest to collective-actor positions and which are left out?

  • Compare party claims and group preferences
  • Unequal proximity versus unequal responsiveness
  • Whose priorities shape political supply?
3   RESHAPING SUPPORT

Can affordable, credible policies broaden support?

Can policy design address both the affordability of present costs and the credibility of future benefits?

  • Two barriers: affordability and credibility
  • Redistribution, explanations and delivery safeguards
  • Test both barriers independently
